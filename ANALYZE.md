# The analysis playbook

Apply to any bill text. Work provision by provision; depth beats breadth.

For each major provision:

**Provision.** Section number and name; quote the operative language
briefly. Never paraphrase statutory text as if quoting; if a section is a
placeholder or reserved, say so.

**Imputed goals [CONFIRM].** One to three, phrased as the drafter would
own them. These go to the member's staff as a question — "these appear to
be your goals; confirm or amend before full analysis" — never as an
assertion.

**Likely effects [TOLD REGARDLESS].** Downstream consequences by
mechanism, including at least one the drafters may not want to hear.
These are surfaced no matter what the confirmed goals are.

**Implementation barriers.** The lobbyist-grade critique, made for the
public instead of a client: which actor bears new administrative burden;
whether participation is optional for an actor whose cooperation the
program needs; whether eligibility carve-outs add complexity that
suppresses take-up; whether statutory specificity (naming a technology, a
dollar figure without indexing, a process) will age badly; what breaks at
scale. Ground every claim in a case-studies/ analogue where one exists.

**Outcome metrics.** Only officially published series — name the agency
and the actual data product. Be honest when the natural outcome is NOT a
tracked series (say "no official series exists; nearest proxy is X, which
misses Y") rather than inventing one. Where a series is
first-print-resolvable, propose the conditional forecast:
"P(metric path | enactment vs not)."

Honesty rules: smaller and well-grounded beats exhaustive and thin; no
invented sections, numbers, or mechanisms; label every judgment as
imputed, likely, or established.
