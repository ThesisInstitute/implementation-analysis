# Emergency savings accounts, SECURE 2.0 (2022)

**Provision.** SECURE 2.0 allowed employers to auto-enroll workers into
pension-linked emergency savings accounts, at employer discretion.

**Intent.** Broaden emergency savings among workers who lack them —
progress against the "can you cover a $500 expense" gap.

**The drafting choice.** Treasury insisted highly compensated employees be
ineligible — an anti-gaming guard on a benefit with little tax advantage
to game (drafters had pushed for no tax advantage at all).

**What happened in practice.** The carve-out forced employers and
recordkeepers to track eligibility by compensation class inside a benefit
that was already optional for them. Administrative burden landed on
exactly the actors whose voluntary cooperation the program needed;
recordkeeper and employer interest collapsed, and take-up with it.

**The lesson.** An eligibility carve-out inside an *optional* program is a
tax on the intermediary who must administer it — if their participation is
voluntary, complexity doesn't narrow the benefit, it kills it.

**Provenance.** Practitioner account of a drafter involved in the
provision (2026-07); take-up and recordkeeper-response characterization to
be corroborated with public reporting and industry data.
