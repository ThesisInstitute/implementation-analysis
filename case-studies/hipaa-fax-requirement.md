# Technology-specific transmission language, HIPAA era

**Provision.** Health-information rules of the HIPAA era named specific
transmission technologies — fax among them — in regulatory text.

**Intent.** Ensure protected health information moved through channels
regarded as secure at the time.

**The drafting choice.** Naming the technology rather than the property
(security/auditability of transmission).

**What happened in practice.** The named technology outlived its
justification by decades; fax persisted in U.S. healthcare workflows long
after better channels existed, contributing to the sector's notorious
information-sharing friction and administrative cost.

**The lesson.** Statutory or regulatory specificity about *means* rather
than *ends* creates implementation debt with no natural sunset — the text
keeps enforcing a world that no longer exists.

**Provenance.** Practitioner shorthand for a well-known pattern
(2026-07); the precise citations (which rule texts named which
technologies, and their amendment history) need verification before this
case is quoted rather than used directionally.
